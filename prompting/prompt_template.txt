### CRITERIA FOR "YES":
The article MUST go beyond being "news" and provide "analytical value" on:
• National-level MAJOR government policies, laws, schemes, regulations, reforms, governance issues
• Constitutional matters, Supreme Court judgments, rights, legal doctrines
• Best practices of public administration, bureaucracy, IAS/IPS roles, governance case studies
• Economy: MACROECONOMIC budgets, economic policies, RBI/NITI reports, inflation/unemployment trends, GDP, taxation, trade, macroeconomic concepts
• International relations: India's relations of MAJOR importance, treaties, MAJOR geopolitical development affecting India, MAJOR events with multilateral institutions (UN, IMF, WTO), global governance

### CRITERIA FOR "NO" (The "UPSC Filter"):
• Local city/state news with no national policy relevance
• Crimes, accidents, lifestyle, entertainment, religion, astrology, sports
• Day-to-day political fights, election gossip related to MP/MLA, political party statements (containing party names like BJP, Congress)
• Stock market movements, FPI flows, rupee fluctuations, company-specific business news

### REASONING PROCESS (Perform these checks sequentially):

1. The Syllabus Tagging Test
Logic: Can you map this article to a SPECIFIC line item in the UPSC Syllabus (e.g., "GS2-RPA Act", "GS3-Cyber Security")?
Signal: If the link is vague, forced, or generic (e.g., linking a random theft to 'Internal Security'), output NO.

2. The Precedent vs. Procedure Test
Logic: Distinguish between a change in law (Precedent) and the usage of law (Procedure).
Signal (YES): Establishes a NEW legal doctrine, strikes down a law, or interprets the Constitution (e.g., Supreme Court Constitution Bench).
Signal (NO): Application of existing law to an individual (e.g., Bail hearings, Parole, FIRs, Raids).

3. The Local Implementation Trap
Logic: Distinguish between National Policy and local infrastructure execution.
Signal (NO): Specific roads, bridges, metro lines, or building inaugurations in a specific city/state (e.g., "Ahmedabad-Mehsana Highway expansion").
Exception (YES): National Strategic Projects (e.g., Border Roads, Inter-linking of Rivers).

4. The Individual Justice Trap
Logic: Distinguish between systemic governance issues and individual crimes.
Signal (NO): Specific arrests, court updates, or investigations involving specific people (e.g., "Abu Salem parole", "MLA arrest", "Tailor arrested for terror", "Closure report in MUDA case").
Rule: Unless the judgment changes the law for everyone, individual cases are NO.

5. The Political Drama Trap
Logic: Distinguish between Governance/Constitutional crises and political infighting.
Signal (NO): Parties blaming each other, Governor returning a bill (if focus is on the row), "Scams" (investigation stage).
Example (NO): "Rajasthan Governor returns honour killing Bill (focus on Gehlot govt context)."

6. The Operational Detail Trap
Logic: Distinguish between Policy decisions and administrative accounting.
Signal (NO): Day-to-day spending, tenders, or administrative status reports.
Example (NO): "MMRDA spends 90cr", "Govt examining fraudulent transactions", "Police inaction on MLA".

7. The 365-Day Shelf Life Test
Logic: Will this information be useful for an exam answer written 6 months from now?
Signal (NO - Transient): Allegations, rumors, meeting dates, protests, or "slams/attacks".
Signal (YES - Enduring): Passed Bills, Official Indices, Committee Reports, or Final Verdicts.

8. The Specific Case Study Exception
Logic: Is this local news a perfect example of Governance Failure or Best Practice?
Rule: Only mark YES if it can be cited as a generic example in an Ethics (GS4) or Governance answer.
Constraint: If unsure, lean NO.

9. The "State-Specific Administrative" Trap
Logic: Distinguish between National Policy frameworks and State/Municipal administrative measures.
Signal (NO): A State Govt or Civic Body launching a specific Helpline, App, Portal, Task Force, or Ward Committee.
Exception (YES): Only if it is a "First of its kind" model explicitly praised by NITI Aayog/Centre for nationwide replication (e.g., "Kerala Model" case studies).

10. The "Project Audit" Trap (CAG/Courts)
Logic: Distinguish between systemic policy failures and specific project irregularities.
Signal (NO): CAG, Courts, or Committees flagging tender violations, cost overruns, or procedural delays in a specific state project (e.g., "Irrigation Project tender scam").
Signal (YES): CAG Reports on Union Finances, Railways, or Defence that highlight macro-level policy gaps.
